   traffic crosses the INTERNET, anyone in the path can read/alter it.
   ═══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════════

   SITE-TO-SITE VPN  (gateway ↔ gateway, always-on, whole networks)
   ┌───────────────┐        ~~~~ untrusted INTERNET ~~~~        ┌───────────────┐
   │ Branch LAN    │   ╔══════════════════════════════════╗   │  HQ LAN        │

   └───────────────┘   ▲ hosts don't know — routers do it ▲   └───────────────┘
                       encrypt at A ───────────► decrypt at B

   REMOTE-ACCESS VPN  (client ↔ gateway, on-demand, one user)
   ┌───────────┐      ~~~~ INTERNET ~~~~      ┌──────────────┐
   │ Laptop    │  ╔═══════ TLS or IKEv2 ═════╗│  VPN gateway │──► corporate LAN
   │ +VPN app  ├─►║ encrypted tunnel          ║│  (headend)   │
   └───────────┘  ╚═══════════════════════════╝└──────────────┘

   WHAT IPsec GIVES YOU (= the CIA triad, over the WAN):
     🔒 Confidentiality (ESP encryption) · ✅ Integrity (HMAC) ·

**One-line mental model:** the internet is a hostile transit network your other controls can't reach, so a **VPN builds an encrypted, authenticated tunnel** across it — **site-to-site** joins two whole *networks* router-to-router, **remote-access** connects one *user's device* to the gateway — and **IPsec** is the framework that does the encrypting, integrity-checking, and peer-authenticating.

## 3. ⚡ TL;DR

- 🟢 A **VPN** = a **v**irtual **p**rivate **n**etwork: a private, encrypted **tunnel** over a shared/public network (the internet), so data crosses untrusted paths safely.
- 🟢 **IPsec provides four services** = the CIA triad on the wire: **Confidentiality** (encryption), **Integrity** (hashing/HMAC), **Authentication** (peer proves identity), **Anti-replay** (sequence numbers).
- 🟢 **IPsec is a framework, not one protocol.** Three parts: **IKE** (negotiate keys/parameters), **ESP** (encrypt + authenticate data), **AH** (authenticate only — no encryption, breaks with NAT → rarely used).
- 🟢 **AH = IP protocol 51** (integrity/auth, no encryption). **ESP = IP protocol 50** (encryption + integrity — the workhorse). **IKE = UDP 500** (+ **UDP 4500** for NAT-T).
- 🟢 **Two modes:** **tunnel** (encrypts the *whole* original packet, new IP header — used for VPNs) vs **transport** (encrypts payload only — host-to-host).
- 🟢 **IKE two phases:** **Phase 1** builds a secure control channel + authenticates peers (via **Diffie-Hellman**, PSK or certificates); **Phase 2** negotiates the actual data-protecting SAs (ESP).
- 🟢 **Site-to-site:** gateway↔gateway, always-on, joins two *networks*, transparent to hosts. **Remote-access:** client↔gateway, on-demand, one *user*, via a software client (IPsec/IKEv2 **or** SSL/TLS).
- 🔵 **Crypto combo:** asymmetric (DH/RSA) to exchange keys & authenticate, then fast symmetric (AES) for the bulk data — same pattern as SSH (9.6).
- 🔗 Ties the whole block together: CIA (9.1), certificates/PKI (9.4), encryption-vs-cleartext (9.6), the trust-boundary theme (9.8–9.10) — now extended across the WAN.

## 4. 🎯 Importance — The Problem First

**The underlying problem:** everything in Block 9 so far protected *inside* a site — hardened logins (9.3–9.6), centralized identity (9.5), a locked-down switched edge (9.8–9.10). But the modern network isn't one site. A branch office needs the HQ's servers; a remote worker needs the corporate LAN from a coffee shop. That traffic must cross the **public internet** — a network you don't own, can't secure, and where *any* device in the path (ISPs, compromised routers, a snooping café Wi-Fi) can read or alter cleartext. None of your L2/L3 controls reach out there. A **VPN** solves this by wrapping the traffic in an **encrypted, authenticated tunnel**, making a hostile transit network behave like a private link.

- 🌍 **This is where the trust boundary leaves the building.** 9.8–9.10 were all about retrofitting *trust boundaries* onto a LAN that trusts too much. The WAN is the ultimate untrusted segment — so instead of *inspecting* traffic (you can't, it's not your network), you **encrypt** it end-to-end. VPN is the trust-boundary theme scaled from "which switch port" to "which continent."
- 🔒 **It's the CIA triad (9.1) enforced across the internet.** A VPN delivers **confidentiality** (nobody in the path can read it), **integrity** (nobody can alter it undetected), and **authentication** (you're really talking to HQ, not an impostor) — the exact guarantees we defined at the start of the block, now applied where you have the *least* control.
- 🧩 **It reuses every crypto idea you've already met.** Symmetric vs asymmetric encryption, key exchange, certificates/PKI (9.4), the "prove identity with keys, don't send secrets" principle (SSH, 9.6) — IPsec assembles all of them into one framework. You're not learning new crypto; you're seeing how the pieces combine into a production system.
- 🏢 **It's foundational to how real networks are built.** Site-to-site VPNs connect branches, data centers, and clouds; remote-access VPNs are how the entire remote workforce reaches the corporate LAN. This concept is the seed of **SD-WAN** (which is IPsec tunnels + intelligent orchestration — your CCNP horizon) and of **zero-trust remote access**. Understanding IPsec here is the base of a huge amount of CCNP Enterprise.
- 💼 **On the job**, you'll build and troubleshoot these constantly — matching Phase-1/Phase-2 parameters, opening UDP 500/4500 + ESP on firewalls, exempting VPN traffic from NAT, choosing IKEv2 over IKEv1, deciding site-to-site vs remote-access vs SSL. The exam wants the *concepts*; the job wants the concepts *plus* the config — so this doc gives you both.

> 🧠 **Anchor:** *"The internet is the untrusted segment — so tunnel and encrypt across it."* A VPN gives you CIA over a network you don't control; IPsec is the framework, and the two shapes are network-to-network (site-to-site) and user-to-network (remote-access).

## 5. 📋 Description

### 5.1 What a VPN is 🟢

- **VPN = Virtual Private Network:** a **logical, encrypted tunnel** carried over a shared/public network, giving the *privacy* of a dedicated link at the *cost* of shared infrastructure.
- **Tunnel = encapsulation:** the original packet is wrapped inside a new, protected packet; only the two endpoints can unwrap it. Everyone in between sees ciphertext.
- **Why not a leased line?** Private WAN links (MPLS, dedicated fiber) are private but expensive and slow to provision. A VPN gives *comparable privacy* over cheap, ubiquitous internet.

### 5.2 The four security services IPsec provides 🟢 — *the CIA payoff*

| Service | What it guarantees | How IPsec does it |
|---|---|---|
| 🔒 **Confidentiality** | Nobody in the path can *read* it | **Encryption** (ESP; symmetric AES) |
| ✅ **Integrity** | Nobody can *alter* it undetected | **Hashing / HMAC** (SHA) |
| 🪪 **Authentication** | The peer is *who it claims* | **PSK** or **digital certificates** (peer auth) |
| 🔁 **Anti-replay** | Old captured packets can't be *re-injected* | **Sequence numbers** |

### 5.3 IPsec is a framework — its three parts 🟢

IPsec (RFC 4301) is an **architecture**, not a single protocol. Three moving parts, routinely confused:

- **IKE (Internet Key Exchange)** — the **negotiator**. A UDP protocol (port **500**) that authenticates the peers and agrees on all the crypto parameters and keys. (Formerly ISAKMP.) *No user data — just setup.*
- **ESP (Encapsulating Security Payload)** — the **workhorse**. **IP protocol 50.** Provides **encryption + integrity + authentication** of the data. This is what protects your actual traffic.
- **AH (Authentication Header)** — **IP protocol 51.** Provides **integrity + authentication only — NO encryption.** It authenticates the payload *and* immutable IP header fields, which means **NAT breaks it** (NAT rewrites addresses → AH sees tampering). Rarely used on internet VPNs; **ESP is almost always the choice.**

### 5.4 Tunnel vs transport mode 🟢

```
   Original packet:  [ IP hdr | payload ]

   TRANSPORT mode:   [ IP hdr | ESP | payload(encrypted) | ESP-trailer ]
      • protects the PAYLOAD only; original IP header stays visible
      • host-to-host; less overhead

   TUNNEL mode:      [ NEW IP hdr | ESP | (ORIGINAL IP hdr + payload)=encrypted | trailer ]
      • protects the ENTIRE original packet (header + payload)
      • gateway-to-gateway / site-to-site; the standard for VPNs
      • the real source/dest are hidden inside the encrypted blob
```
- **Tunnel mode** is what site-to-site (and most remote-access) VPNs use: it hides the internal addressing entirely.

### 5.5 The IKE phases 🟢🔵

IKE builds the tunnel in **two phases**:

- **Phase 1 — build the secure *control* channel (IKE/ISAKMP SA).**
  - Peers authenticate each other (**pre-shared key** or **digital certificate**) and run a **Diffie-Hellman** exchange to derive shared keys *without sending them*.
  - Negotiates (IKEv1 mnemonic **HAGLE**): **H**ash, **A**uthentication, **G**roup (DH), **L**ifetime, **E**ncryption.
  - IKEv1 uses **Main Mode** (6 messages, protects identities) or **Aggressive Mode** (3 messages, faster, exposes identities).
  - *Deliverable:* one encrypted, authenticated channel — **no user data yet.**
- **Phase 2 — negotiate the *data*-protecting tunnel (IPsec SA).**
  - Over the secure Phase-1 channel, agree the **ESP** parameters (encryption/integrity algorithms) that actually protect user traffic. (IKEv1 calls this **Quick Mode**.)
  - Optional **PFS (Perfect Forward Secrecy)** = a fresh DH exchange so compromising one key doesn't expose past/future sessions.
- 🔵 **IKEv2** collapses this into a leaner, more reliable exchange (IKE_SA_INIT + IKE_AUTH), supports **EAP** (great for remote-access), and is the modern default — prefer it over IKEv1.

### 5.6 Site-to-site vs remote-access 🟢 — *the two the blueprint names*

| | 🏢 **Site-to-Site** | 💻 **Remote-Access** |
|---|---|---|
| Connects | two **networks** (LAN ↔ LAN) | one **user's device** ↔ the network |
| Endpoints | **gateway ↔ gateway** (routers/firewalls) | **client software ↔ VPN gateway (headend)** |
| Who builds the tunnel | the **routers** (hosts unaware) | the **VPN app** on the user's device |
| State | **always-on** (permanent) | **on-demand** (user connects when needed) |
| Typical tech | IPsec (tunnel mode) | IPsec/**IKEv2** *or* **SSL/TLS** VPN |
| Example | branch office ↔ HQ | work-from-home laptop ↔ corporate LAN |

- 🔵 **Remote-access flavours:** classic **IPsec client** (IKEv2) vs **SSL/TLS VPN** (e.g. Cisco **Secure Client / AnyConnect**) which rides **TLS over 443** — firewall-friendly (looks like HTTPS), no special client-side IPsec config, ideal for roaming users.

## 6. ⚙️ How It Works — Under the Hood

### 6.1 Bringing a site-to-site tunnel up (the sequence)

```
   1. "INTERESTING TRAFFIC" — a packet from Branch LAN to HQ LAN matches the
      VPN's crypto ACL → "this must be protected" → triggers the tunnel.
   2. IKE PHASE 1 (UDP 500): the two routers authenticate (PSK/cert) and run
      Diffie-Hellman → a shared secret + an encrypted control channel (IKE SA).
   3. IKE PHASE 2 (over that channel): negotiate ESP parameters → the IPsec SAs
      that will actually encrypt user data (one SA per direction).
   4. DATA TRANSFER: packets are ESP-encrypted at Router A, sent across the
      internet as ciphertext, decrypted at Router B, delivered to HQ LAN.
   5. REKEY / TEARDOWN: SAs expire on their lifetime → renegotiate; idle → tear down.
```
Key mental split: **IKE (control plane, UDP 500/4500)** sets everything up; **ESP (data plane, IP proto 50)** carries the protected traffic. Two totally different kinds of traffic for one tunnel.

### 6.2 The crypto combo (why both kinds of encryption) 🔵

```
   ASYMMETRIC (slow, but no shared secret needed):
     Diffie-Hellman → derive a shared key WITHOUT sending it over the wire
     RSA / certificates → prove each peer's identity (authentication)
        │
        ▼  (used once, during setup)
   SYMMETRIC (fast, for bulk data):
     AES → encrypt the millions of actual data packets with the shared key
```
This is the **exact pattern from SSH (9.6)** and TLS: expensive asymmetric crypto to *bootstrap trust and exchange a key*, then cheap symmetric crypto to *move the data*. Certificates here are the same PKI/X.509 identities from 9.4.

### 6.3 NAT-T — surviving NAT 🔵

ESP (IP protocol 50) isn't TCP/UDP, so NAT devices (which rewrite TCP/UDP ports) can't track it and often drop it. **NAT Traversal (NAT-T, RFC 3948)** detects NAT during IKE and **wraps ESP inside UDP 4500**, making it NAT-friendly. This is why firewalls for IPsec must permit **UDP 500 (IKE)**, **UDP 4500 (NAT-T)**, and **IP protocol 50 (ESP)**.

### 6.4 Config sketch — site-to-site IKEv1 (🔵 background; 5.5 is *describe*)

```
! (1) Phase 1 policy — MUST MATCH on both peers
crypto isakmp policy 10
 encr aes 256              ! encryption
 hash sha256              ! integrity
 authentication pre-share ! peer auth method
 group 14                 ! Diffie-Hellman (2048-bit) — min recommended
 lifetime 86400
crypto isakmp key S3cr3tK3y address 203.0.113.1     ! PSK for the peer

! (2) Phase 2 — transform set (ESP algorithms)
crypto ipsec transform-set TSET esp-aes 256 esp-sha256-hmac
 mode tunnel

! (3) "interesting traffic" — which flows get encrypted
access-list 100 permit ip 192.168.1.0 0.0.0.255 192.168.2.0 0.0.0.255

! (4) crypto map ties it together, applied to the WAN interface
crypto map CMAP 10 ipsec-isakmp
 set peer 203.0.113.1
 set transform-set TSET
 match address 100
interface GigabitEthernet0/0
 crypto map CMAP

! ⚠️ also: EXEMPT VPN traffic from NAT, and permit UDP500/4500 + ESP on the firewall
! verify: show crypto isakmp sa (Phase 1) / show crypto ipsec sa (Phase 2 + packet counts)
```

### 6.5 Verify 🟢

```
Router# show crypto isakmp sa      ! Phase 1 state (QM_IDLE = up)
Router# show crypto ipsec sa       ! Phase 2 SAs + encrypt/decrypt packet counters
Router# debug crypto isakmp        ! watch Phase 1 negotiation (mismatch hunting)
Router# debug crypto ipsec         ! watch Phase 2
```
Rising **encrypt/decrypt counters** in `show crypto ipsec sa` = the tunnel is actually passing protected traffic (not just "up" but *working*).

## 7. ⚖️ Pros & Cons

**👍 Strengths**
- ✅ **Private communication over cheap public internet** — CIA across a network you don't own.
- ✅ **Standards-based & interoperable** (IPsec is IETF) — multi-vendor site-to-site works.
- ✅ **Transparent (site-to-site)** — hosts need no client; the routers do the work.
- ✅ **Flexible (remote-access)** — IPsec/IKEv2 or firewall-friendly SSL/TLS for roaming users.
- ✅ **Foundation for SD-WAN / scalable WAN** — the building block of modern enterprise WAN.

**👎 Limits**
- ⚠️ **Configuration complexity** — Phase 1/Phase 2 parameters must **match exactly** on both peers; a single mismatch = no tunnel (the classic troubleshooting grind).
- ⚠️ **NAT & firewall friction** — ESP+NAT needs NAT-T; firewalls must permit UDP 500/4500 + ESP; VPN traffic must be NAT-exempted.
- ⚠️ **Overhead** — encryption + encapsulation add latency and bytes (MTU/fragmentation issues).
- ⚠️ **Site-to-site doesn't scale by hand** — a full mesh of N sites = N² tunnels → this is *exactly* why **DMVPN/SD-WAN** exist (CCNP).
- ⚠️ **It protects data *in transit* only** — not endpoints; a compromised laptop with a VPN just gives the attacker an encrypted tunnel *into* your LAN.

## 8. 🧬 Variants (Common → Specific)

- **By design:** **site-to-site** (gateway↔gateway) → **remote-access** (client↔gateway).
- **By remote-access transport:** **IPsec/IKEv2** client → **SSL/TLS VPN** (Cisco Secure Client/AnyConnect, TLS 443) → **clientless SSL** (browser portal, limited).
- **By IPsec protocol:** **ESP** (encrypt+auth, standard) → **AH** (auth only, NAT-breaking, rare).
- **By mode:** **tunnel** (VPN standard) → **transport** (host-to-host).
- **By IKE version:** **IKEv1** (Main/Aggressive) → **IKEv2** (leaner, EAP, modern default).
- **By peer auth:** **pre-shared key** (simple, small scale) → **digital certificates / PKI** (scalable, 9.4).
- 🔵 **Scaling & CCNP horizon:** **GRE-over-IPsec** (carry routing/multicast) → **DMVPN** (dynamic spoke-to-spoke) → **GET-VPN** → **FlexVPN (IKEv2)** → **SD-WAN** (IPsec + orchestration). *These are your ENCOR/ENSDWI future.*

## 9. 🧰 Devices / Media / Protocols

- **VPN gateways / headends** — routers, firewalls (ASA/FTD), dedicated concentrators that terminate tunnels.
- **VPN client software** — Cisco **Secure Client (AnyConnect)** and OS-native IKEv2 clients for remote-access.
- **The IPsec protocol suite:** **IKE** (UDP **500**), **NAT-T** (UDP **4500**), **ESP** (IP protocol **50**), **AH** (IP protocol **51**).
- **Crypto building blocks:** **Diffie-Hellman** (key exchange), **AES** (symmetric bulk), **RSA/ECDSA + X.509 certs / PKI** (auth, from 9.4), **SHA/HMAC** (integrity).
- **Firewall requirements:** permit **UDP 500 + UDP 4500 + IP proto 50 (ESP)**; **NAT-exempt** VPN traffic.
- **IOS CLI (background):** `crypto isakmp policy`, `crypto isakmp key`, `crypto ipsec transform-set`, `crypto map`, `show crypto isakmp sa`, `show crypto ipsec sa`.
- 🔗 **Related standards:** RFC 4301 (IPsec architecture), RFC 7296 (IKEv2), RFC 3948 (NAT-T).

*(Devices carry no scope markers — universal.)*

## 10. ✅ Best Practices

- 🔐 **Prefer IKEv2** over IKEv1 — simpler, more reliable, EAP support, better crypto agility.
- 🧊 **Use strong, modern crypto** — **AES-256**, **SHA-256+**, **DH group 14** (or ECDH 19/20); retire DES/3DES/MD5/DH1-2.
- 🪪 **Use certificates over PSKs at scale** — PKI (9.4) for many peers; PSKs only for small/simple deployments.
- 🎯 **Prefer ESP, not AH** — you almost always want confidentiality, and AH breaks with NAT.
- 🧮 **Match Phase 1/Phase 2 parameters exactly** on both peers — the #1 cause of tunnels that won't come up.
- 🌐 **Open the right firewall holes** — UDP 500/4500 + ESP — and **NAT-exempt** the VPN flows.
- 📏 **Plan MTU** — account for IPsec overhead (adjust `ip tcp adjust-mss` / MTU) to avoid fragmentation.
- 🔁 **Enable PFS & sensible rekey lifetimes** — fresh keys limit the blast radius of a key compromise.
- 💻 **For remote-access, prefer SSL/TLS (Secure Client) for roaming users** — traverses NAT/firewalls as HTTPS; pair with **MFA** (9.4) and **AAA** (9.5) on the headend.
- 🧱 **Remember it's transit-only** — combine VPN with endpoint security, AAA, and least-privilege; a tunnel from a compromised device is a tunnel *for the attacker*.

## 11. 🚫 No-Goes (Mistakes, Traps & Troubleshooting)

**Common mistakes**
- ❌ **Mismatched Phase 1/Phase 2 parameters** — encryption/hash/DH/lifetime/auth must match on both ends or the tunnel never forms.
- ❌ **Forgetting NAT exemption** — VPN traffic gets NATed and no longer matches the crypto ACL → tunnel "up" but no traffic passes.
- ❌ **Firewall blocking UDP 500/4500 or ESP** — IKE never completes or data never flows.
- ❌ **Using AH where NAT exists** — AH + NAT = broken by design; use ESP.
- ❌ **Non-mirrored crypto ACLs** — the "interesting traffic" ACLs must be exact mirror images on each peer.
- ❌ **Weak/legacy crypto** — DES/3DES/MD5/DH group 1 — deprecated and crackable.
- ❌ **Treating "tunnel up" as "working"** — Phase 1 up ≠ data flowing; check Phase 2 + encrypt/decrypt counters.
- ❌ **Confusing site-to-site with remote-access** — different endpoints (gateway vs client), different use cases.

**🌳 Decision tree — "the VPN won't work"**

```
No tunnel at all?
│
├─ Phase 1 not coming up ('show crypto isakmp sa' → nothing / MM_NO_STATE)
│     → mismatched Phase-1 policy (encr/hash/DH/auth/lifetime)? PSK mismatch?
│       Is UDP 500 reaching the peer (routing/firewall/NAT)?  → fix the mismatch/path.
│
├─ Phase 1 up but Phase 2 fails ('show crypto ipsec sa' → no SA)
│     → mismatched transform-set, or non-mirrored crypto ACLs. Align them.
│
└─ Both phases up but NO DATA passes
      → NAT not exempting VPN traffic (traffic NATed → doesn't match crypto ACL),
        OR MTU/fragmentation, OR firewall dropping ESP. Check encrypt/decrypt counters.

Which VPN type do I need?
   join two whole networks, always-on, hosts unaware   → SITE-TO-SITE
   one user's device connects on demand                → REMOTE-ACCESS (IKEv2 or SSL/TLS)
```

**🕵️ Dirty Secrets**
- 🤫 **90% of "broken IPsec" is a parameter mismatch, and the error messages are useless.** Both ends must agree on encryption, hash, DH group, lifetime, auth, *and* mirror-image crypto ACLs — miss one and the tunnel silently won't form, often with a cryptic `MM_NO_STATE` or nothing at all. Seasoned engineers troubleshoot IPsec by *diffing the two configs line-by-line*, not by reading logs. Knowing that saves hours.
- 🤫 **"Tunnel is up" is the great false comfort.** `show crypto isakmp sa` showing `QM_IDLE` means Phase 1 succeeded — the *control channel* — and tells you **nothing** about whether user data is flowing. People declare victory at Phase 1 and then wonder why nothing works; the real test is rising **encrypt/decrypt counters** in `show crypto ipsec sa`. Phase 1 up + zero data = a Phase-2 or NAT problem.
- 🤫 **NAT is IPsec's arch-nemesis, and it kills tunnels three different ways.** AH is *destroyed* by NAT (it authenticates the IP header NAT rewrites). ESP (proto 50) has no ports for NAT to track, so it gets dropped — hence NAT-T wrapping it in UDP 4500. And even with NAT-T, forgetting to *exempt* your VPN traffic from the router's own NAT means it gets translated *before* encryption and no longer matches the crypto ACL. Three separate NAT footguns, and every one produces a different failure.
- 🤫 **AH is in every textbook and almost never in production.** The exam makes you learn AH vs ESP, but on real internet VPNs AH is essentially dead — it doesn't encrypt (pointless when you want privacy) and NAT breaks it. If you need "auth without encryption" you use ESP-NULL, not AH. Learn AH for the exam; expect to configure ESP for the rest of your career.
- 🤫 **A VPN protects the pipe, not the people at the ends.** The single biggest misconception: "we have a VPN, we're secure." A VPN encrypts data *in transit* — it does nothing for a malware-infected laptop, which now has an encrypted, trusted tunnel *straight into your LAN*. This is precisely why the industry is moving from "VPN = trusted once connected" to **zero-trust** (authenticate and authorize *every* request, VPN or not). The tunnel is necessary, not sufficient.
- 🤫 **Site-to-site doesn't scale, and that single fact created an entire industry.** Manually meshing N sites needs N×(N-1)/2 tunnels, each hand-configured and matched — unmanageable past a handful of sites. **DMVPN** (dynamic tunnels), then **SD-WAN** (IPsec + centralized orchestration + intelligent path selection) exist *entirely* to solve the scaling pain you'll feel the first time you configure your third or fourth site-to-site tunnel by hand. That pain is the on-ramp to your whole CCNP Enterprise journey.

## 13. 🧪 Practical Tasks

> 5.5 is a *describe* objective → tasks blend **concept drills** with an optional **hands-on build** (engineer depth).

1. 🧭 **Site-to-site vs remote-access sort.** Given 10 scenarios (branch↔HQ, WFH laptop, two data centers, a contractor's tablet…), classify each and justify endpoints (gateway vs client), always-on vs on-demand, and tunnel tech. → *The core 5.5 distinction.*
2. 🧱 **Map the four services to CIA.** For confidentiality/integrity/authentication/anti-replay, name the IPsec mechanism (ESP/HMAC/PSK-or-cert/sequence numbers) and the 9.1 CIA leg it serves. → *Ties the block together.*
3. 🔧 **Build a site-to-site tunnel (Packet Tracer 8 / GNS3).** Two routers over a simulated internet: Phase-1 policy, PSK, transform set, crypto ACL, crypto map; ping across; verify `show crypto isakmp sa` + `show crypto ipsec sa` (watch encrypt/decrypt counters). → *Makes the phases real.*
4. 🧨 **Break-and-fix parameter mismatch.** Deliberately mismatch a Phase-1 DH group (or PSK) and watch Phase 1 fail; then a transform-set mismatch to fail Phase 2; diff the configs to find each. → *Builds the real IPsec troubleshooting reflex.*
5. 🌐 **NAT-exemption trap.** Add NAT on the routers *without* exempting VPN traffic; observe the tunnel "up" but no data; add the NAT exemption and watch it flow. → *The #1 silent failure, felt.*
6. 🔐 **Crypto choices.** Compare AES-256/SHA-256/DH14 vs legacy DES/MD5/DH1; argue why the modern set and why IKEv2 over IKEv1. → *Reasoning from crypto principles (9.4).*
