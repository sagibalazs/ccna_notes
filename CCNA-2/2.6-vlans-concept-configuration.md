## 2️⃣ Visualization — Big Picture at a Glance

**One physical switch → many logical, isolated broadcast domains:**

```
        WITHOUT VLANs                          WITH VLANs
  ┌──────────────────────────┐        ┌──────────────────────────┐
  │        ONE switch         │        │        ONE switch         │
  │  ┌────────────────────┐   │        │  ┌────────┐  ┌────────┐   │
  │  │ ONE broadcast domain│  │        │  │VLAN 10 │  │VLAN 20 │   │
  │  │  every port hears   │  │        │  │ (Sales)│  │ (Eng)  │   │
  │  │  every broadcast    │  │        │  └────────┘  └────────┘   │
  │  └────────────────────┘   │        │   isolated    isolated    │
  │  PC PC PC PC PC PC PC PC   │        │  PC PC PC     PC PC PC     │
  └──────────────────────────┘        └──────────────────────────┘
   • 1 broadcast domain                 • 2 broadcast domains on ONE box
   • usually 1 IP subnet                 • usually 2 IP subnets
   • no L2 isolation                     • VLAN 10 ≠ VLAN 20 (need a router to cross → 2.10)
```

**A VLAN spanning two switches (needs a TRUNK — mechanism in 2.8):**

```
  SW1                                   SW2
  VLAN10─Fa0/1 (PC-A)                    Fa0/1 (PC-C)─VLAN10
  VLAN20─Fa0/2 (PC-B)                    Fa0/2 (PC-D)─VLAN20
             │                                   │
           Gi0/1 ══════ TRUNK (802.1Q) ══════ Gi0/1
                   carries VLAN10 + VLAN20, each frame TAGGED with its VLAN ID

  Result: PC-A (SW1/VLAN10) and PC-C (SW2/VLAN10) are in the SAME broadcast domain,
          even across two switches — because the trunk tags & carries VLAN 10.
```

**VLAN ID map (memorize the ranges):**

| Range | IDs | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| 🟢 **Default** | **1** | every port starts here; can't delete/rename |
| 🟢 **Normal** | **1–1005** | day-to-day / CCNA; stored in **vlan.dat** |
| 🟢 **Reserved** | **1002–1005** | legacy Token Ring/FDDI; can't delete/use |
| 🔵 **Extended** | **1006–4094** | large/SP networks; VTPv3 or transparent mode |
| ⛔ **Reserved by 802.1Q** | **0 & 4095** | not usable (12-bit VID = 0–4095) |

## 3️⃣ TL;DR

- 🟢 A **VLAN (Virtual LAN)** is a **logical broadcast domain** created in switch software — it splits one physical switch into several independent Layer-2 networks. **1 VLAN ≈ 1 broadcast domain ≈ 1 IP subnet.**
- 🟢 VLANs give **segmentation without extra hardware**: security/isolation, smaller broadcast domains, and logical grouping independent of physical location.
- 🟢 **Traffic cannot cross between VLANs at Layer 2** — it needs a **router / L3 switch** (inter-VLAN routing → 2.10). That isolation is the whole point.
- 🟢 **VLAN 1** is the default; **every port belongs to VLAN 1** until reassigned. VLANs **1, 1002–1005** can't be deleted.
- 🟢 **Ranges:** normal **1–1005** (CCNA/day-to-day), extended **1006–4094** (large networks). The 802.1Q **VID is 12 bits** → usable **1–4094**.
- 🟢 **Configure:** `vlan <id>` + `name <n>` to create; on the port `switchport mode access` **then** `switchport access vlan <id>`. **Verify:** `show vlan brief`.
- 🟢 **A VLAN spanning multiple switches needs a TRUNK** between them (802.1Q tagging → 2.8). **Trunk ports do NOT appear in `show vlan brief`.**
- 🔵 **Dirty secret:** normal-range VLANs live in **`vlan.dat`** in flash, *not* `running-config` — so `erase startup-config` + reload **does not** remove them.

---

## 4️⃣ Importance — The Problem First 🎯

**The underlying problem:** From 2.4, a switch **floods** broadcasts, multicasts, and unknown unicasts to **every port in the domain**. On a flat switched network that means **one giant broadcast domain**: every ARP, every DHCP Discover, every broadcast storm reaches **every** device. That creates four concrete pains:

1. 📈 **Broadcast load** — the more hosts in one domain, the more broadcast noise every host (and CPU) must process. It doesn't scale.
2. 🔓 **No isolation** — Finance, Guests, and IoT cameras all share one L2 space; anyone can reach anyone, and one compromised host can sniff/attack the whole segment.
3. 📍 **Physical = logical** — grouping is dictated by *where a cable plugs in*, not by *what a device is for*. Moving a person's department means recabling.
4. 💥 **Blast radius** — a loop or storm in one flat domain takes down **everyone**.

**VLANs solve all four by making the broadcast domain a *software* property of the port, not a wiring fact:**

- ❓ *"How do I shrink broadcast domains without buying more switches?"* → put ports in **different VLANs**; each VLAN floods only within itself.
- ❓ *"How do I isolate groups on the same hardware?"* → different VLANs can't reach each other at L2 (need a controlled L3 hop → 2.10).
- ❓ *"How do I group by role, not location?"* → assign any port to any VLAN regardless of where it physically is (even across switches, via trunks).

**Why an engineer must own this cold:**

- 🔧 **VLANs are the backbone of every campus design** — data/voice/guest/mgmt separation, per-department subnets, security zones. Almost every later Block-2/9 topic (trunking, STP-per-VLAN, inter-VLAN routing, ACLs, DHCP snooping) is *scoped by VLAN*.
- 🔧 **"Host can't communicate" is very often a VLAN problem** — wrong access VLAN, VLAN missing from the database, or VLAN not carried on a trunk. Your first three troubleshooting commands live here.
- 🔧 **The vlan.dat gotcha bites real engineers** — "I erased the config and reloaded, why are the VLANs still here?" Understanding storage prevents hours of confusion (and botched factory resets).

## 5️⃣ Description 📋

- 🟢 **What a VLAN is:** a **logical grouping of switch ports** into an independent **broadcast domain**. Frames in VLAN 10 are only ever flooded/forwarded among VLAN 10 ports; VLAN 10 and VLAN 20 are as isolated as if they were on two physically separate switches.
- 🟢 **VLAN ≈ subnet:** by near-universal convention, each VLAN maps to **one IP subnet**. Devices in a VLAN talk directly (L2); reaching another VLAN requires **routing** (→ 2.10).
- 🟢 **Per-VLAN everything:** the switch keeps a **separate MAC table context per VLAN** (from 2.5) and runs **per-VLAN STP** (Rapid PVST+ → 2.18). A VLAN is a full, self-contained L2 world.
- 🟢 **VLAN membership (how a port joins a VLAN):**
  - **Static (port-based)** — admin assigns the port to a VLAN (the standard, CCNA-relevant method).
  - 🔵 **Dynamic** — assigned by policy (e.g., MAC-based via a server) — rare, beyond CCNA.
- 🟢 **VLAN ID ranges:**
  - **Normal range 1–1005** — everyday use; stored in **`vlan.dat`** (flash).
  - **Reserved 1002–1005** — legacy Token Ring/FDDI (`fddi-default`, `token-ring-default`, `fddinet-default`, `trnet-default`); auto-created, **cannot be deleted or used**.
  - **Extended range 1006–4094** — for large enterprises/service providers; needs **VTP transparent mode** (VTPv1/2) or **VTPv3** (→ 2.12).
  - **802.1Q VID = 12 bits** → 0–4095, with **0 and 4095 reserved** → usable **1–4094**.
- 🟢 **Default VLAN = VLAN 1:** created automatically, named `default`, **every port belongs to it** until reassigned. **Cannot be deleted or renamed.** Best practice: move user/mgmt traffic *off* VLAN 1.
- 🟢 **Spanning multiple switches:** the same VLAN ID on two switches is the *same* broadcast domain **only if a trunk carries it between them**. The trunk **tags** each frame with its VLAN ID (802.1Q → 2.8). Access links (to end hosts) are **untagged**; trunk links (switch-to-switch) are **tagged**.
- 🔵 **Storage nuance:** normal-range VLANs persist in **`vlan.dat`**, *not* in `running-config`/`startup-config`. A true factory reset requires deleting `vlan.dat` **and** `startup-config`, then reloading.
- 🟢 **Deleting a VLAN:** any **access ports** still assigned to it go **inactive** (they keep the dead VLAN ID and stop passing traffic) until reassigned — a classic "why did that port die?" trap.

## 6️⃣ How It Works — Under the Hood ⚙️

### A) 🏷️ The core idea — a VLAN tag colors each frame

```
Inside the switch, every frame is associated with a VLAN ID ("color"):

  PC-A on Fa0/1 (access VLAN 10) sends a frame
        │
        ▼
  Switch tags it internally as "VLAN 10"
        │
        ├─ flooding/forwarding happens ONLY among VLAN-10 ports
        │
        ▼
  • If it exits an ACCESS port → tag is STRIPPED (hosts never see tags)
  • If it exits a TRUNK port   → tag is WRITTEN into the frame (802.1Q) so the
                                 next switch knows it's VLAN 10  (→ 2.8)
```

The end host is blissfully unaware VLANs exist — it just sees ordinary Ethernet. VLAN membership is entirely a **switch-side** decision, enforced on ingress by the **access VLAN** of the port.

### B) 🧱 Where VLANs live & how the config maps

```
VLAN DATABASE (vlan.dat, flash)          RUNNING-CONFIG (per interface)
  vlan 10  name SALES                       interface Gi0/1
  vlan 20  name ENGINEERING                    switchport mode access
  (normal-range VLAN definitions)              switchport access vlan 10
                                            interface Gi0/2
  ⚠ NOT in running-config →                    switchport mode access
    survives 'erase startup-config'            switchport access vlan 20
```

Two halves: the **VLAN definitions** (IDs + names, in the database) and the **port memberships** (in the interface config). Both must be right for a host to work.

### C) 🛠️ Configuration flow (the exact order matters)

```
! 1) Create & name the VLAN (enters the VLAN in the database)
Switch(config)# vlan 10
Switch(config-vlan)# name SALES
Switch(config-vlan)# exit

! 2) Assign an access port  — set MODE first, THEN the VLAN
Switch(config)# interface Gi0/1
Switch(config-if)# switchport mode access        ← declare it an access (end-host) port
Switch(config-if)# switchport access vlan 10      ← put it in VLAN 10
Switch(config-if)# exit

! Range assign many ports at once
Switch(config)# interface range Gi0/2 - 12
Switch(config-if-range)# switchport mode access
Switch(config-if-range)# switchport access vlan 20
```

> 🔑 **Order rule:** set `switchport mode access` **before** `switchport access vlan`. Do it backwards and behavior can be unpredictable (the port may still negotiate/behave as dynamic). Also: if the VLAN doesn't exist yet, most IOS versions **auto-create** it when you assign it — but create it explicitly so it gets a proper name.

### D) 🔍 Verification — read the state like an engineer

```
Switch# show vlan brief
VLAN Name                 Status    Ports
---- -------------------- --------- -------------------------------
1    default              active    Gi0/13, Gi0/14, Te1/0/1, Te1/0/2   ← leftovers in VLAN 1
10   SALES                active    Gi0/1                              ← our access port
20   ENGINEERING          active    Gi0/2, Gi0/3, ... Gi0/12
50   IT                   active                                       ← VLAN exists, no ports yet
1002 fddi-default         active
1003 token-ring-default   active
1004 fddinet-default      active
1005 trnet-default        active
```

- **VLAN | Name | Status | Ports.** Status `active` = usable (`act/lshut` = admin-shut locally).
- ⚠️ **Trunk ports are ABSENT here.** A trunk isn't a member of one VLAN, so it never shows in the Ports column — use `show interfaces trunk` for that (→ 2.8). **This is one of the most-tested `show vlan` facts.**

```
Switch# show interfaces Gi0/1 switchport      ← per-port truth: admin vs operational mode, access VLAN
Switch# show interfaces status                 ← Port | Status | Vlan(or 'trunk') | Duplex | Speed
Switch# show vlan id 10                         ← everything about VLAN 10
Switch# show mac address-table interface Gi0/1  ← which MAC/VLAN is actually seen on the port
```

### E) 🌐 Spanning switches — same ID, one domain (via trunk)

```
For VLAN 10 to be one broadcast domain across SW1 and SW2:
  1. VLAN 10 must exist in BOTH switches' databases.
  2. A TRUNK link must connect them AND allow VLAN 10.
  3. The trunk tags VLAN-10 frames (802.1Q) so each switch keeps them in VLAN 10.

Miss any of these → SW1-VLAN10 and SW2-VLAN10 are ISLANDS that can't talk.
(Mechanism of tagging/trunk negotiation = 2.8; native VLAN = 2.9.)
```

> 🕵️ **Dirty secret — the silent VLAN mismatch.** If VLAN 10 exists on SW1 but the trunk to SW2 doesn't allow it (or SW2 lacks VLAN 10), hosts in "VLAN 10" on each switch simply can't reach each other, with **no error** — everything shows `up/up`. Always verify with `show vlan brief` on *both* ends **and** `show interfaces trunk`.

## 7️⃣ Pros & Cons ⚖️

| ✅ Pros | ❌ Cons / Costs |
|---|---|
| 🟢 **Segmentation without new hardware** — one switch = many broadcast domains. | 🔵 **Inter-VLAN traffic needs L3** — a router/L3 switch and extra config (→ 2.10). |
| 🟢 **Smaller broadcast domains** → less broadcast load, better performance. | 🔵 **More moving parts** — VLAN DB, trunks, native VLAN, per-VLAN STP to keep consistent. |
| 🟢 **Security/isolation** — separate Finance/Guest/IoT/mgmt at L2. | ⚠️ **Misconfig = silent failure** (VLAN not on trunk, wrong access VLAN) — hard to spot. |
| 🟢 **Logical grouping** independent of physical location (spans switches via trunk). | ⚠️ **vlan.dat persistence** surprises people during resets/reuse. |
| 🟢 **Scales to 4094 VLANs**; per-VLAN policy (STP, ACLs, QoS). | 🔵 **VLAN sprawl** — undocumented VLANs pile up; needs governance (naming/VTP). |

## 8️⃣ Variants — Common → Specific 🌿

**By purpose (typical campus VLANs):**
- 🟢 **Data VLAN** — normal user/host traffic (the default case).
- 🟢 **Voice VLAN** — IP phone traffic on an access port (deep dive → 2.7).
- 🟢 **Management VLAN** — switch mgmt (SSH/SNMP); keep it *off* VLAN 1.
- 🟢 **Native VLAN** — the untagged VLAN on a trunk (→ 2.9).
- 🔵 **Guest / Quarantine / Black-hole VLAN** — isolation zones.

**By ID range:**
- 🟢 **Default (1)** · 🟢 **Normal (1–1005)** · 🟢 **Reserved (1002–1005)** · 🔵 **Extended (1006–4094)**.

**By membership method:**
- 🟢 **Static / port-based** — admin-assigned (CCNA focus).
- 🔵 **Dynamic** — policy/MAC-based (e.g., via a VMPS/ISE) — beyond CCNA.

**By reach:**
- 🟢 **Local** — VLAN exists on one switch.
- 🟢 **Spanned** — same VLAN across switches over trunks (this doc's headline case).

## 9️⃣ Devices / Media / Protocols 🧰

**Devices:**
- 🟢 **[Layer-2 Switch](./devices/switch.md)** — creates/enforces VLANs; keeps per-VLAN MAC tables & STP.
- 🟢 **[Router / L3 Switch](./devices/router.md)** — the *only* way VLANs interconnect (inter-VLAN routing → 2.10); an SVI gives a VLAN its L3 gateway.
- 🟢 **[Endpoint](./devices/nic.md#endpoint-nic)** — unaware of VLANs on a normal access port (sees plain Ethernet).
- 🟢 **[IP Phone](./devices/nic.md#endpoint-nic)** — special case: data + voice VLAN on one access port (→ 2.7).

**Protocols / features scoped by or enabling VLANs:**
- 🟢 **[802.1Q](./protocols/dot1q.md)** — the tag that lets a VLAN cross a trunk (→ 2.8).
- 🔵 **[VTP](./protocols/vtp.md)** — syncs the normal-range VLAN database between switches (→ 2.12).
- 🟢 **[STP/RSTP](./protocols/stp.md)** — runs **per VLAN** (Rapid PVST+ → 2.18).
- 🔵 **DHCP / ARP** — operate **within** a VLAN's broadcast domain (→ IP Services).
- 🔵 **ACLs / DHCP snooping / DAI** — applied per-VLAN/per-port for security (→ Block 9).

## 🔟 Best Practices ✅

- 🟢 **Don't use VLAN 1 for user, management, or native traffic.** Create dedicated VLANs and move everything off VLAN 1 (a long-standing security best practice).
- 🟢 **Always set `switchport mode access` before `switchport access vlan`** on host ports (order + explicit mode avoids surprise negotiation).
- 🟢 **Name every VLAN meaningfully** (`SALES`, `VOICE`, `MGMT`) — unnamed VLANs (`VLAN0010`) breed confusion and sprawl.
- 🟢 **Verify on both ends when spanning switches** — `show vlan brief` on each switch **and** `show interfaces trunk` to confirm the VLAN is carried.
- 🔵 **Document the VLAN↔subnet map.** Treat "VLAN 10 = 10.10.10.0/24 = Sales" as authoritative design data.
- 🔵 **Reassign ports before deleting a VLAN** so they don't silently go inactive.
- 🔵 **Remember vlan.dat for resets/reuse.** To truly wipe a switch: `erase startup-config` **and** delete `vlan.dat`, then `reload`.
- 🔵 **Prune unused VLANs from trunks** (housekeeping + reduced broadcast scope) — detail in 2.8.

## 1️⃣1️⃣ No-Goes — Common Mistakes & Troubleshooting Traps 🚫

- ❌ **Expecting different VLANs to talk at Layer 2.** They **can't** by design — you need a router/L3 switch (→ 2.10). If two hosts "in different VLANs can't ping," that's *working as intended* until you add routing.
- ❌ **Thinking a trunk shows up in `show vlan brief`.** It doesn't — trunk ports aren't listed under any VLAN's Ports column. Use `show interfaces trunk`.
- ⚠️ **Assigning an access VLAN but forgetting the VLAN must exist / be carried on the trunk.** Same VLAN ID on two switches ≠ connected unless a trunk **allows** it. Silent failure, all `up/up`.
- ⚠️ **`switchport access vlan` in the wrong order / without `mode access`.** May leave the port dynamic/negotiating; set the mode first.
- ⚠️ **Deleting a VLAN with live access ports.** Those ports go **inactive** and stop forwarding until reassigned — a self-inflicted outage.
- 🔵 **"I erased the config but VLANs remain."** Normal-range VLANs are in **`vlan.dat`**, not `running-config`. Delete `vlan.dat` too.
- 🔵 **Trying to delete/rename VLAN 1 or 1002–1005.** Not allowed — they're immutable defaults/reserved.
- ⚠️ **Leaving management on VLAN 1.** Security risk and best-practice violation; give management its own VLAN.

## 1️⃣3️⃣ Practical Tasks 🛠️

> Listed briefly; expanded step-by-step later in context.

1. 🟢 **Create & assign (Packet Tracer 8 / GNS3).** Make VLAN 10 `SALES` and VLAN 20 `ENG`; put two PCs in each; verify with `show vlan brief`. Confirm same-VLAN PCs ping and cross-VLAN PCs **don't**.
2. 🟢 **Range assignment.** Use `interface range` to drop 10 ports into one VLAN at once; verify.
3. 🟢 **Verify per port.** `show interfaces <int> switchport` and `show interfaces status` — read the operational mode and access VLAN; contrast an access port vs a trunk (`Vlan = trunk`).
4. 🟢 **Prove the "trunk absent" fact.** Configure a trunk, then run `show vlan brief` and confirm the trunk port is **not** listed under any VLAN; find it with `show interfaces trunk`.
5. 🔵 **Span a VLAN across two switches.** Put VLAN 10 on both; trunk them; verify PC-A (SW1) ↔ PC-C (SW2) in VLAN 10 communicate. Then remove VLAN 10 from one switch and watch it silently break.
6. 🔵 **The vlan.dat experiment.** Create VLANs, `erase startup-config`, `reload` — observe the VLANs **survive** (`show vlan brief`). Then delete `vlan.dat` and reload to truly clear them.
7. 🔵 **Delete-a-live-VLAN trap.** Assign a port to VLAN 30, then delete VLAN 30; observe the port go **inactive**; fix by reassigning.
