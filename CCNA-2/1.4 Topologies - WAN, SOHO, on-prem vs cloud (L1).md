## 3 · TL;DR ⚡

- **WAN (`[1.2.d]`)** connects **geographically separate sites** (HQ, branches, DC, cloud) over **provider infrastructure you rent** — MPLS L3VPN, Internet VPN, Metro Ethernet, broadband, **4G/5G**, DWDM. A **router/NGFW** sits at each edge; increasingly an **SD‑WAN** overlay blends multiple transports with central policy.
- 🔑 **WAN layout is a cost‑vs‑directness trade:** **hub‑and‑spoke** (all branches to HQ — cheap, central policy, but HQ is a chokepoint and spoke‑to‑spoke detours through it) · **full mesh** (every site to every site — direct, but **N²** links, expensive) · **partial mesh** (the pragmatic middle).
- **SOHO (`[1.2.e]`)** = small office / home office: **everything in one box** (modem + router + switch + firewall + Wi‑Fi AP). A **flat** network, one/few subnets, minimal redundancy, consumer gear. Simple and cheap — but a **single point of failure** with no real segmentation.
- **On‑prem vs cloud (`[1.2.f]`)** is *who owns the infrastructure*: **on‑prem** = you own the DC (**capex**, full **control**, you maintain and carry the scaling limits); **cloud** = you **rent** from AWS/Azure/GCP (**opex**, near‑infinite **elastic** scale, provider maintains).
- **Cloud service models** = how much you manage: **IaaS** (VMs/network — you manage OS‑up) → **PaaS** (platform — you deploy apps) → **SaaS** (finished software — you just use it). **Deployment models:** private / public / **hybrid** / multicloud.
- **Cloud connects to the WAN** via site‑to‑site **VPN**, **Direct Connect/ExpressRoute**, or **cloud on‑ramp** (SD‑WAN) — so "on‑prem vs cloud" is usually **hybrid** in practice.
- 🔑 **The through‑line:** WAN answers **where** (across distance), SOHO answers **how small** (one box), cloud answers **who owns it** (you vs a provider). All three are decisions about **reach, scale, and ownership** — not about the internal LAN structure (that's `1.3`).

---

## 4 · Importance — the problem first 🎯

**The problem:** `1.3` structured the network **inside** a site — campus hierarchy, DC fabric. But real organizations aren't a single site, don't all want to **own** their infrastructure, and aren't all big. Three different pressures push past the campus:

1. **Distance.** A LAN can't span a city, a country, or a continent — Ethernet reaches ~100 m (`1.3`/media). Yet a company has a **head office, branches, a data center, and cloud workloads** that must all talk. **You can't run your own cable between cities.** → the **WAN** connects them over **infrastructure a service provider owns and you rent.**
2. **Scale (downward).** Not every "network" is a campus. A **home office or a two‑person shop** needs connectivity too, but a three‑tier design would be absurd overkill. → the **SOHO** topology collapses everything — router, switch, firewall, Wi‑Fi — into **one affordable box.**
3. **Ownership.** Building and running a data center is **expensive, slow, and rigid** (buy servers, power, cooling, staff; over‑provision for peak). Many workloads would rather **rent** compute that scales on demand. → the **cloud** lets you use someone else's infrastructure (**opex, elastic**) instead of owning it (**capex, control**) — and **hybrid** mixes both.

So these three topologies are about **reach, size, and ownership**:
- **WAN** — because the business is **spread across distance** and you can't own the links between sites.
- **SOHO** — because the network is **small enough** that one box is the right answer.
- **On‑prem vs cloud** — because **owning vs renting** infrastructure is now a real, consequential choice.

Remove them and you can build a beautiful campus that **can't reach the branch office, can't serve the home worker, and must own every server it ever uses.** These topologies are how a network stops being one building and becomes an **organization** — distributed, right‑sized, and increasingly **hybrid** between what you own and what you rent.

🔵 **Why the depth?** Because the consequential decisions here are **economic and architectural at once**: which WAN transport and layout (cost vs directness vs resilience), whether a site is small enough for SOHO, and — the big one — **on‑prem vs cloud vs hybrid** (capex/opex, control, elasticity, latency, compliance). Getting these wrong is expensive in a way a mis‑wired switch never is.

## 5 · Description 📋

**WAN (`[1.2.d]`)** 🟢 — a network spanning **geographic distance**, built on **service‑provider transport** (you rent the links between sites, unlike the LAN you own):
- **Transports:** leased line, **MPLS L3VPN**, Metro Ethernet, **Internet VPN**, broadband, **4G/5G**, DWDM.
- **Layouts:** point‑to‑point, **hub‑and‑spoke**, **full mesh**, **partial mesh**.
- **Edge device:** a **router** (`1.1.7`) and/or **NGFW** (`1.1.8`) terminates each site's WAN link.
- **SD‑WAN:** an **overlay** across multiple transports with **central policy** (CCNP ENSDWI).

**SOHO (`[1.2.e]`)** 🟢 — small office / home office: a single **all‑in‑one device** (often modem + router + switch + firewall + Wi‑Fi AP fused). **Flat** network, one/few subnets, minimal redundancy, consumer/prosumer gear. The bottom rung of the router/firewall descents from `1.1.7`/`1.1.8`.

**On‑premises vs cloud (`[1.2.f]`)** 🟢:
- **On‑premises** — you **own and operate** the data center: **capex**, full control, you maintain it, you carry the scaling limits.
- **Cloud** — you **rent** from a provider (AWS/Azure/GCP): **opex**, elastic scale, the provider maintains it.
- **Service models:** **IaaS** (infrastructure) → **PaaS** (platform) → **SaaS** (software) — you manage progressively less.
- **Deployment models:** private / public / **hybrid** / multicloud.
- **Connectivity:** VPN, **Direct Connect/ExpressRoute**, **cloud on‑ramp** (ties cloud to the WAN). *Deep service‑model/virtualization detail: `7.4`, Block 7.*

## 6 · How It Works — each topology ⚙️

### 6.1 WAN — transport you rent, laid out for the traffic 🟢
```
Site A ── CE router ──[ SERVICE PROVIDER: MPLS / Internet / Metro-E / 5G / DWDM ]── CE router ── Site B
          (you own the edge)     (the provider owns the middle — you rent it)     (you own the edge)

LAYOUTS:
  hub-and-spoke : every branch → HQ. Cheap, central policy. BUT HQ is a chokepoint,
                  and spoke-to-spoke traffic detours through HQ.
  full mesh     : every site ↔ every site. Direct any-to-any. BUT N sites = N(N-1)/2 links → expensive.
  partial mesh  : mesh the sites that need direct paths; hub-and-spoke the rest. The pragmatic middle.
  SD-WAN        : overlay tunnels over ALL available transports (MPLS + Internet + 5G),
                  steer traffic by policy/app, fail over between links. Central controller.
```

### 6.2 SOHO — one box does everything 🟢
```
ISP ─► [ modem │ router │ switch │ firewall │ Wi-Fi AP ]  ─► a few wired + wireless clients
                      (all fused into a single consumer device)
   • one flat subnet (e.g. 192.168.1.0/24)   • NAT to one public IP   • minimal/no redundancy
```
Simple, cheap, trivial to run — but **flat** (no segmentation) and a **single point of failure**.

### 6.3 On‑prem vs cloud — own vs rent 🟢
```
ON-PREM: you buy servers/power/cooling/staff, run your own DC → capex, control, you maintain, fixed capacity
CLOUD:   you rent compute/storage/network from a provider → opex, elastic, provider maintains, scales on demand

SERVICE MODELS (control ↓ / convenience ↑):
  IaaS  → you get VMs/network; you manage OS, runtime, apps          (most control)
  PaaS  → you deploy apps onto a managed platform                    (middle)
  SaaS  → you just use finished software (email, CRM)                (least to manage)

DEPLOYMENT: private (your cloud) · public (shared provider) · HYBRID (both) · multicloud (several providers)
CONNECT:    site-to-site VPN · Direct Connect/ExpressRoute (private circuit) · cloud on-ramp (SD-WAN)
```

### 6.4 ⚡ Design relationships (the trade‑offs) 🔑
> These topologies are economic and architectural choices at once. These are the levers.

**① WAN layout ↔ cost vs directness vs resilience.** **Hub‑and‑spoke** is cheapest and centrally managed but makes **HQ a chokepoint** and forces **spoke‑to‑spoke traffic to detour** through it. **Full mesh** gives direct any‑to‑any paths but costs **N(N‑1)/2 links** — it explodes with site count. **Partial mesh** meshes only where direct paths matter. *Buy directness/resilience with links and money — spend it where the traffic and the outages justify it.*

**② You rent the WAN middle ↔ SLA, cost, control.** Unlike the LAN (you own every metre), the WAN's transport is the **provider's** — so you're buying **bandwidth, latency, and an SLA**, not owning them. *The WAN's economics and reliability are contractual, not physical — pick transports (MPLS vs Internet vs 5G) by cost/SLA, not just speed.*

**③ SD‑WAN ↔ transport independence.** SD‑WAN overlays tunnels across **any** transports and steers per‑app by policy → you can add **cheap Internet/5G** alongside (or instead of) pricey MPLS and **fail over** between them. *It decouples your policy from the underlying carrier links — cheaper, more resilient, centrally controlled.*

**④ SOHO ↔ simplicity vs no redundancy/segmentation.** One box is cheap, simple, and enough for a handful of users — but it's a **single point of failure**, a **flat** network with **no segmentation**, and consumer‑grade. *SOHO trades resilience and structure for cost and simplicity — correct for a home, wrong for a campus.*

**⑤ On‑prem vs cloud ↔ capex/opex & control/elasticity.** **On‑prem** = big upfront **capex**, full **control**, but **fixed** capacity you must over‑provision. **Cloud** = **opex**, near‑infinite **elastic** scale, but less control and new concerns (egress cost, latency to cloud, compliance). **Hybrid** splits the difference. *It's an ownership‑and‑elasticity trade — "cloud is cheaper" is not automatically true once egress, latency, and compliance are counted.*

**⑥ Service model ↔ how much you manage.** **IaaS → PaaS → SaaS** hands progressively more of the stack to the provider (you manage less, control less). *Pick the model by how much you want to run yourself vs consume — more convenience means less control.*

**⑦ Cloud connectivity ↔ the WAN.** Cloud isn't a separate island — it attaches to your WAN via **VPN, Direct Connect/ExpressRoute, or cloud on‑ramp (SD‑WAN)**. *"On‑prem vs cloud" is almost always **hybrid**, and the WAN is how the two halves connect* (§6.1, §6.3).

**⑧ Distance ↔ why a WAN exists at all.** A LAN stops at ~100 m of copper / campus fiber (`1.3`/media); anything farther needs **provider transport**. *The WAN exists because geography exceeds what you can cable yourself — it's the topology of distance.*

## 7 · Pros & Cons ⚖️

| Topology | ✅ Strengths | ⚠️ Trade‑offs |
|---|---|---|
| **WAN hub‑and‑spoke** | Cheap; central policy; simple | HQ chokepoint; spoke‑to‑spoke detours (§6.4 ①) |
| **WAN full mesh** | Direct any‑to‑any | N² links; costly/complex |
| **WAN partial mesh / SD‑WAN** | Balanced; transport‑independent; resilient | More design/config effort |
| **SOHO** | One box; cheap; trivial to run | Flat; no redundancy/segmentation (§6.4 ④) |
| **On‑prem** | Full control; data locality; predictable | Capex; fixed capacity; you maintain it |
| **Cloud** | Opex; elastic; provider‑maintained | Less control; egress cost; latency/compliance (§6.4 ⑤) |
| **Hybrid** | Best‑of‑both; workload‑placed flexibly | Two environments to manage/secure/connect |

## 8 · Variants — transports & models 🌿

- **WAN transports:** leased line → **MPLS L3VPN** → Metro Ethernet → **Internet VPN** / broadband → **4G/5G** → DWDM.
- **WAN layouts:** point‑to‑point → **hub‑and‑spoke** → partial mesh → **full mesh**; overlay = **SD‑WAN** (CCNP ENSDWI).
- **SOHO:** consumer all‑in‑one → prosumer (separate AP/switch) — the small‑network spectrum.
- **Cloud service models:** **IaaS → PaaS → SaaS** (manage less each step).
- **Cloud deployment models:** **private → public → hybrid → multicloud**.
- **Cloud connectivity:** site‑to‑site **VPN → Direct Connect/ExpressRoute → cloud on‑ramp** (SD‑WAN).

## 10 · Best Practices ✅

- **Match WAN layout to the traffic and budget:** hub‑and‑spoke for central apps; (partial) mesh where spoke‑to‑spoke matters; SD‑WAN to blend transports and add resilience (§6.4 ①③).
- **Choose WAN transport by cost + SLA, not just speed** — you're renting bandwidth and a contract (§6.4 ②).
- **Use SOHO only where it fits** — a home/tiny office; never as an enterprise design (flat, no redundancy) (§6.4 ④).
- **Decide on‑prem vs cloud on the full picture:** capex/opex, control, elasticity, latency, egress cost, compliance — **hybrid is often the honest answer** (§6.4 ⑤).
- **Pick the service model by how much you want to manage** (IaaS↔SaaS) (§6.4 ⑥).
- **Plan cloud connectivity as part of the WAN** (VPN / Direct Connect / on‑ramp), not as an afterthought (§6.4 ⑦).
- **Deep cloud/virtualization decisions live in Block 7 (`7.4`)** — here, decide the topology; there, the internals.

## 11 · No‑Goes 🚫

- ❌ **Full‑meshing a large WAN "for redundancy."** N² links = cost/complexity explosion; use partial mesh / SD‑WAN (§6.4 ①).
- ❌ **Treating a SOHO all‑in‑one as an enterprise design.** Flat, no redundancy, one failure domain (§6.4 ④).
- ❌ **Assuming cloud is automatically cheaper/better.** Egress, latency, and compliance can flip the math (§6.4 ⑤).
- ❌ **Ignoring the HQ chokepoint** in hub‑and‑spoke when spoke‑to‑spoke traffic is heavy (§6.4 ①).
- ❌ **Buying WAN transport on headline speed alone** — the SLA and cost model matter more (§6.4 ②).
- ❌ **Forgetting cloud must attach to the WAN** — it's not a standalone island (§6.4 ⑦).
- ❌ **Re‑covering the campus/DC hierarchy here** — that's `1.3 [1.2.a–c]`.
