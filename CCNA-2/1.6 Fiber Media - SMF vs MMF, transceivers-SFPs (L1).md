## 3 · TL;DR ⚡

- **Fiber carries data as light** through a glass **core** wrapped in a **cladding** (lower refractive index) that traps the light by **total internal reflection**. It's **immune to EMI**, **secure** (no electrical emanation to tap), and has **enormous bandwidth/reach** — but **can't carry power (no PoE)** and costs more than copper (`1.5`).
- 🔑 **The star relationship — core size decides distance (modal dispersion).** **MMF** has a **big core (50/62.5 µm)** → light takes **many paths (modes)** → the paths arrive at slightly different times → the pulse **spreads (modal dispersion)** → **short reach (~hundreds of m)**. **SMF** has a **tiny core (~9 µm)** → **one path** → **no modal dispersion** → **kilometres**. *The whole SMF‑vs‑MMF distance gap is modal dispersion.*
- **Light source differs:** MMF uses cheap **LEDs/VCSELs** at **850 nm**; SMF uses a precise **laser** at **1310/1550 nm**. 🔑 **That transceiver — not the glass — is the real SMF cost premium.**
- **Grades:** MMF **OM3/OM4/OM5** (OM1/2 legacy); SMF **OS2** (backbone/outdoor). Jacket colours: OM1/2 orange, OM3/4 aqua, OM5 lime, SMF yellow.
- **Transceivers/SFPs** convert electrical ↔ optical and set speed + reach: **SFP** (1G) → **SFP+** (10G) → **SFP28** (25G) → **QSFP+/28/DD** (40/100/400G). **Optic types:** **SR** (MMF, short), **LR** (SMF, long), **ER/ZR** (SMF, extra‑long). 🔑 **The transceiver, the fiber grade, and the reach must all match.**
- **Connectors:** **LC** (small, modern SFP) · **SC** (square) · **ST** (bayonet, legacy) · **MPO/MTP** (multi‑fiber, 40/100G parallel). Polish: **UPC** (flat) vs **APC** (8° angled, green, lower reflection).
- **Use fiber** where distance exceeds copper's 100 m, where EMI is severe, where security matters, or where bandwidth demands it; **SMF (OS2)** for backbone/hard‑to‑recable runs, **MMF (OM4)** for intra‑building/DC.

---

## 4 · Importance — the problem first 🎯

**The problem:** copper (`1.5`) got you 100 m of cheap, PoE‑capable connectivity — but a network needs to reach **much farther, much faster, through much worse conditions**, and sometimes **must not be tappable or disturbed by electrical noise at all**. Copper's electrical signalling can't:

1. **Span real distance.** Buildings are hundreds of metres apart; campuses and carriers span **kilometres**. Copper dies at 100 m. → **fiber** carries light with **far lower attenuation** (~0.2 dB/km for SMF vs copper's steep loss) → **tens of kilometres**.
2. **Ignore electrical noise entirely.** In factories, hospitals, near power lines, copper errors from EMI no matter how well twisted. → **fiber carries light, not electricity** → it's **completely immune to EMI** and **emits nothing** to interfere with anything else.
3. **Carry the highest bandwidths.** Copper's electrical signalling caps in the low GHz; light runs at **hundreds of THz**. → fiber is the medium of **10/40/100/400G** backbones and data centers.
4. **Be secure.** Copper radiates a signal that can be tapped without cutting it; fiber emits **no electromagnetic field** → tapping it means **physically breaking the light path** (detectable). → fiber for **high‑security** links.

But fiber makes its own trade: it **can't carry power** (no PoE — that stays copper, `1.2`/`1.5`), it's **more fragile** (bend‑sensitive), and it costs more — mostly because of the **precision optics (lasers)** that light single‑mode glass. So the engineer's job is to **choose the right medium for the run**: cheap copper for access, MMF for intra‑building/DC, SMF for backbone and distance.

And there's a second decision fiber forces that copper doesn't: **which transceiver.** A fiber strand is inert glass — the **transceiver (SFP/QSFP)** is what turns electrical bits into light of the right **wavelength and power** for the right **fiber type and reach**. Pick an **SR** optic (for MMF) and plug it into **SMF**, or an **LR** into **MMF**, and the link **won't come up** — a classic, maddening fault. Understanding **modal dispersion, grades, optic types, and matching** is what makes fiber deployable rather than mysterious.

🔵 **Why the depth?** Because "why won't this fiber link come up / why is it slow at distance" almost always traces to a **mismatch**: wrong fiber grade for the speed/distance, wrong optic for the fiber (SR on SMF), a dirty connector, or MMF where SMF was needed. The **modal‑dispersion** insight alone explains most of the distance numbers on every fiber datasheet.

## 5 · Description 📋

**How fiber is built** 🟢 — light travels in the **core**, confined by the **cladding** (lower refractive index) via **total internal reflection**. Notation **core/cladding** in µm (cladding always **125 µm**): **9/125** (SMF), **50/125** & **62.5/125** (MMF).

**Single‑mode (SMF)** 🟢 — **~9 µm** core, **laser** source, **1310/1550 nm**, grades **OS1** (indoor) / **OS2** (outdoor/backbone), **yellow** jacket, **10–80+ km**.

**Multimode (MMF)** 🟢 — **50 µm** (OM3/4/5) or **62.5 µm** (OM1/2) core, **LED/VCSEL** source, **850 nm**, grades **OM1–OM5**, **≤~400 m**.

**Transceivers / SFPs** 🟢🔵 — pluggable optics that convert electrical ↔ optical:
- **Form factors:** **SFP** (1G) · **SFP+** (10G) · **SFP28** (25G) · **QSFP+** (40G) · **QSFP28** (100G) · **QSFP‑DD/OSFP** (400G). (Cages homed in `1.1.5`.)
- **Optic types (speed + reach):** **SR** (Short Reach, MMF, 850 nm) · **LR** (Long Reach, SMF, 1310 nm) · **ER/ZR** (Extended/very‑long, SMF, 1550 nm).

**Connectors** 🟢 — **LC** (small, duplex, modern SFP) · **SC** (square push‑pull) · **ST** (bayonet, legacy) · **MPO/MTP** (8/12/24‑fiber, parallel 40/100G). Polish: **UPC** (flat, blue) vs **APC** (8° angled, green, lower back‑reflection).

## 6 · How It Works — the physics & the optics ⚙️

### 6.1 Total internal reflection & modal dispersion (the star) 🔑
```
CLADDING (index n2, lower)
════════════════════════════
CORE (index n1, higher)   ← light stays trapped by TOTAL INTERNAL REFLECTION
════════════════════════════
CLADDING

MULTIMODE (big core 50/62.5 µm):        SINGLE-MODE (tiny core ~9 µm):
 light enters at MANY angles             only ONE angle fits
 → MANY PATHS (modes)                    → ONE PATH
 → each mode travels a DIFFERENT length  → all light travels the SAME length
 → the pulse SPREADS = MODAL DISPERSION  → NO modal dispersion
 → limits distance (~hundreds of m)      → kilometres, huge bandwidth
```
🔑 A **bigger core** admits light at **many angles**; each **mode** zig‑zags a **different total distance**, so a single pulse **smears out** over the run (**modal dispersion**) until pulses overlap and become unreadable → **short reach**. A **tiny core** admits **one mode** → the pulse stays sharp → **long reach**. *Core size → number of modes → modal dispersion → maximum distance.*

### 6.2 Light source, wavelength & attenuation 🔵
- **MMF** uses cheap **LEDs/VCSELs** at **850 nm**; **SMF** uses a precise **laser** at **1310/1550 nm** aimed into a 9 µm core.
- **Longer wavelengths attenuate less:** SMF at **1550 nm** loses ~**0.2 dB/km**; MMF at 850 nm loses much more (~3 dB/km). *Lower attenuation is why SMF goes farthest.*

### 6.3 Transceivers: matching optic ↔ fiber ↔ reach 🔑
```
The fiber is inert glass; the TRANSCEIVER lights it. It must match the fiber type and reach:
  SR (Short Reach)  → MMF, 850 nm   → tens–hundreds of m (DC, intra-building)
  LR (Long Reach)   → SMF, 1310 nm  → ~10 km (campus/backbone)
  ER / ZR           → SMF, 1550 nm  → 40–80+ km (long haul)
FORM FACTOR sets speed: SFP(1G) · SFP+(10G) · SFP28(25G) · QSFP+(40G) · QSFP28(100G) · QSFP-DD(400G)
⚠️ An SR optic on SMF (or LR on MMF) will NOT link — the optic is built for one core/wavelength.
```

### 6.4 Connectors & polish 🟢
**LC** (small, dominant on SFP) · **SC** (square) · **ST** (bayonet, legacy) · **MPO/MTP** (multi‑fiber ribbon for 40/100G parallel optics). **APC** (angled, green) gives lower back‑reflection than **UPC** (flat) — don't mate APC to UPC.

### 6.5 ⚡ Fiber relationships (the "why") 🔑
> The glass and the optic are both part of the link; these relationships decide the design.

**① Core size ↔ modes ↔ modal dispersion ↔ distance.** (§6.1) The master fiber relationship — MMF's big core trades distance for cheap optics; SMF's tiny core buys kilometres. *Distance is bought with a smaller core (and a pricier laser).*

**② Light source ↔ media ↔ cost.** MMF uses cheap **LEDs/VCSELs**; SMF needs a precise **laser** into a 9 µm core → **SMF transceivers cost more**. This — not the glass — is the real SMF‑vs‑MMF cost driver. *The transceiver, not the fibre, is where SMF costs you.*

**③ Wavelength ↔ attenuation ↔ reach.** Longer wavelengths (**1550 nm**) attenuate **less** → SMF's long wavelengths enable long haul; MMF's 850 nm suits short runs. *The colour of the light is a reach decision* (§6.2).

**④ Transceiver ↔ fiber ↔ reach — all three must agree.** 🔑 An **SR** optic needs **MMF**; **LR/ER** need **SMF**; the **form factor** (SFP/QSFP) sets the speed. Mismatch any one and the link **won't come up**. *A fiber link needs the optic, the fibre grade, and the reach all matching — three things, not one* (§6.3; cage in `1.1.5`).

**⑤ Grade ↔ speed at distance.** OM3 does 10G@300 m; OM4 @400 m; 40/100G on MMF reach only ~100–150 m; **OS2 SMF** reaches km. *Higher speed shortens MMF reach hard — that's modal dispersion again at higher frequency* (§6.1).

**⑥ EMI immunity & security ↔ light not electricity.** Fiber carries **light** → **immune to EMI** and **emits nothing to tap**. *In noisy or high‑security environments, fiber isn't better — it's the only honest choice* (contrast copper, `1.5`).

**⑦ No PoE ↔ light carries no power.** Fiber can carry a device's **data** but never its **watts** → powered devices stay on copper (`1.2`/`1.5`). *Power can force copper even where fiber's distance/bandwidth would win.*

**⑧ Distance ↔ cost (the design call).** Pick the cheapest medium that reliably covers the distance: **copper** ≤100 m, **MMF (OM4)** intra‑building/DC, **SMF (OS2)** backbone/outdoor/hard‑to‑recable. *For risers and inter‑building runs you can't easily replace, SMF is the conservative long‑term bet even if today's speed is modest.*

## 10 · Best Practices ✅

- **Match transceiver ↔ fiber ↔ reach exactly** — SR on MMF, LR/ER on SMF; form factor to the port cage (§6.5 ④; `1.1.5`).
- **Use OM4 (or OM5) for new MMF; OS2 for new SMF** — never install OM1/OM2 (§8, §6.5 ⑤).
- **Choose SMF for anything hard to re‑cable** (risers, inter‑building, outdoor) even if today's speed is modest — conservative long‑term bet (§6.5 ⑧).
- **Use fiber where EMI is severe, distances are long, or security matters** (§6.5 ⑥).
- **Keep copper for PoE** — powered devices need twisted pair (§6.5 ⑦; `1.2`).
- **Label jackets by colour** (yellow=SMF, aqua=OM3/4, lime=OM5, orange=OM1/2) and **match connector/polish** (don't mate APC↔UPC) (§6.4).
- **Keep connectors clean** — dust/oil on a ferrule is a top cause of fiber loss/errors (sets up `1.8`).
- **Respect bend radius** — fiber is bend‑sensitive; tight bends cause loss.

---

## 11 · No‑Goes 🚫

- ❌ **Plugging an SR optic onto SMF (or LR onto MMF).** The optic is built for one core/wavelength — the link won't come up or errors badly (§6.5 ④).
- ❌ **Installing OM1/OM2 for new work.** Legacy; can't carry modern speeds at distance (§8).
- ❌ **Expecting 40/100G MMF to reach far** — it's only ~100–150 m (modal dispersion at high speed) (§6.5 ⑤).
- ❌ **Mating APC to UPC connectors** — high loss/reflection; match polish (§6.4).
- ❌ **Assuming fiber carries PoE.** It carries data only — powered devices need copper (§6.5 ⑦).
- ❌ **Dirty or over‑bent fiber** — a top cause of loss/errors; clean and respect bend radius (§10).
- ❌ **Covering copper or shared‑vs‑p2p here** — those are `1.5` and `1.7`.

## 13 · Practical Tasks 🧪

1. **Read the jacket** 🟢 — identify fiber grade by jacket colour (yellow=SMF, aqua=OM3/4, lime=OM5, orange=OM1/2) and note the core/cladding notation (9/125 vs 50/125) (§2, §8).
2. **Modal‑dispersion reasoning** 🔑 — explain why a 50 µm core caps distance and a 9 µm core doesn't; relate it to why 40/100G MMF reaches only ~100–150 m (§6.1, §6.5 ⑤).
3. **Transceiver ↔ fiber match** 🔑 — given an SR (MMF) and an LR (SMF) SFP+ and two fiber runs, pair them correctly and predict what happens if swapped (§6.3, §6.5 ④).
4. **Reach/optic pick** 🔵 — choose fiber grade + optic type for (a) two DC racks 30 m apart at 100G, (b) two buildings 3 km apart at 10G; justify (§6.5 ①③⑤).
5. **Cost reasoning** 🔵 — explain why SMF links cost more than MMF, and why it's the transceiver (laser) not the glass (§6.5 ②).
6. **Connector/polish** 🟢 — identify LC vs SC vs MPO and UPC vs APC; explain why you never mate APC to UPC (§6.4).
7. **PoE forces copper** 🔵 — a ceiling AP 60 m away needs power + data: why can't fiber do it alone? (§6.5 ⑦; `1.2`).
