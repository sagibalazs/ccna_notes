```
              │  a password ALONE is one weak factor
              ▼
   THE THREE AUTHENTICATION FACTOR CATEGORIES
   ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
     🧠 Type 1  KNOWLEDGE   "something you KNOW"   password, PIN
     🔑 Type 2  POSSESSION  "something you HAVE"   token, smart card, phone, FIDO key

        (+ context: "somewhere you are" / "something you do")

              │  MFA = 2+ factors from DIFFERENT categories
              ▼
   STRENGTH LADDER (weak → strong)
   ══════════════════════════════════════════════════════════
   password only  →  password + SMS OTP  →  password + TOTP/push
        →  🛡 PHISHING-RESISTANT: certificate (PKI/X.509) · FIDO2/passkey
           (private key never leaves hardware; origin-bound; no shared secret)
```

**One-line mental model:** a password policy hardens the *weakest* factor (what you **know**); real strength comes from **adding independent factors** (what you **have**, what you **are**) — and the strongest of those are **cryptographic** (certs, FIDO2), because they can't be phished or replayed.

## 3. ⚡ TL;DR

- 🟢 A **password policy** has two halves: **management** (the credential's *lifecycle* — length, storage, rotation, reuse, lockout) and **complexity** (its *composition*).
- 🟢 **Modern best practice flipped the old rules:** favour **length** (passphrases) over forced character-mix; **stop mandatory periodic rotation** (change only on compromise); **screen against breached-password lists**. (NIST SP 800-63B Rev 4, 2024.)
- 🟢 **Three factor categories:** **Knowledge** (know), **Possession** (have), **Inherence/biometric** (are). **MFA = ≥2 from *different* categories.**
- 🟢 **Certificates (PKI / X.509):** cryptographic proof of identity via a public/private key pair — no shared secret to steal → **phishing-resistant**.
- 🟢 **Biometrics:** authenticate by physical/behavioural traits; graded by **FAR** (false accept = security), **FRR** (false reject = usability), **CER** (crossover — lower is better).
- 🔵 **Phishing-resistant MFA** (certificates, **FIDO2/passkeys**) is the peak: the private key never leaves hardware and auth is bound to the real site, defeating phishing/MITM.
- ⚠️ **A strong hash of a weak password is still weak** (the 9.3 bridge). And **you can't reissue your fingerprint** — biometrics are an identifier, not a secret.
- 🔧 On Cisco gear, local policy knobs are thin (`security passwords min-length`, `login block-for`); *real* MFA/cert/biometric enforcement arrives via **AAA + 802.1X** (9.5).

## 4. 🎯 Importance — The Problem First

**The underlying problem:** in 9.3 we stored credentials with strong hashes — but hashing only protects the *stored* form. It does nothing if the *password itself* is guessable, reused from a breach, phished, or shoulder-surfed. Passwords are a fundamentally weak factor: humans pick predictable ones, reuse them everywhere, and hand them to attackers who ask nicely. A **password policy** squeezes the most safety out of that weak factor; **password alternatives** reduce or remove reliance on it entirely.

- 🧠 **This is the "authenticate" half of access control, done right.** 9.3 gave you the *mechanism* (a secret on a gate). 9.4 asks the harder question: *what makes a credential actually strong, and what's better than a password?*
- 🔄 **Most breaches are credential breaches.** Stolen, reused, phished, or sprayed passwords dominate real incident reports — not zero-day exploits (recall 9.1's Dirty Secret). Get authentication right and you close the single largest door.
- 🧪 **The "obvious" rules were wrong — and knowing *why* is the engineer's edge.** For 20 years policy meant "8 chars, upper+lower+digit+symbol, change every 90 days." Behavioural research showed this *backfires*: it produces `P@ssw0rd1 → P@ssw0rd2` patterns and reused variants. NIST reversed it. If you can explain *why length beats complexity and why forced rotation harms security*, you're reasoning from principles, not repeating folklore — exactly the level this curriculum targets.
- 🛡️ **Phishing-resistance is the modern bar.** MFA that can still be phished (SMS OTP, push-fatigue) is being pushed aside by **cryptographic** factors (certificates, FIDO2) where there's *no shared secret to steal and the auth is bound to the real site*. Knowing which MFA is phishing-resistant vs. phishable is a real design decision you'll make.
- 💼 **On the job**, you'll write/enforce these policies, choose an MFA method, and — critically for a network engineer — deploy the *network* side of strong auth: **802.1X with EAP-TLS certificates**, **RADIUS to an MFA server**, PKI for device identity. That machinery is 9.5, but the *why* is here.

> 🧠 **Anchor:** *"Harden the weak factor, then stop relying on it."* Length + breach-screening make the password less bad; MFA and cryptographic factors make a stolen password *not enough*.

## 5. 📋 Description

### 5.1 Password policy — MANAGEMENT (the lifecycle) 🟢

Everything that governs a credential from birth to death:

- **Length** — the single biggest strength lever. Minimum **8** (NIST floor), **15+ recommended** (CISA); systems must *accept* **64+**.
- **Storage** — irreversible, salted, iterated/memory-hard hash (Cisco type **8/9**; general web: Argon2id/bcrypt/PBKDF2). *(mechanism = 9.3.)*
- **Rotation** — 🔥 **modern rule: don't force periodic change.** Rotate **only** on evidence of compromise. Forced 90-day rotation causes predictable increments and *weakens* security.
- **Reuse / history** — prevent reusing the same or trivially-varied password; ban org-wide reuse across systems.
- **Breach screening** — check new passwords against **known-breached/blocklist** databases at creation; reject "already leaked."
- **Rate-limiting / lockout** — throttle or lock after repeated failures (NIST: friction after ~10 fails) → defeats online guessing.
- **Recovery / reset** — secure reset flows; **no** "security questions" or hints (guessable/searchable).
- **No paste-blocking** — allow paste so password managers (which enable *long, unique* passwords) work.

### 5.2 Password policy — COMPLEXITY (composition) 🟢 — *the reversal*

| | 🕰️ Old (legacy) policy | ✅ Modern (NIST 800-63B Rev 4) |
|---|---|---|
| Character mix | *Require* upper+lower+digit+symbol | **No** mandatory composition rules |
| Length | 8, rarely more | Length-first; **passphrases** encouraged |
| Rotation | Every 30/60/90 days | **Only on compromise** |
| Screening | none | **Screen against breach lists** |
| Hints / Q&A | common | **Prohibited** |
| Charset | often ASCII-only, no spaces | **All Unicode + spaces**, allow paste |

🔵 **Why the flip? Entropy + human behaviour.**
- **Entropy** ≈ how many guesses to crack; it grows with *length* far faster than with a few forced symbols. A 4-word passphrase (`correct-horse-battery-staple`) has more entropy *and* is easier to remember than `P@ssw0rd!`.
- Composition rules push users into *predictable* patterns (capital first, `1` and `!` last), shrinking the real search space. Attackers know the patterns.
- Forced rotation → `Summer2024! → Summer2025!` — minimal change, and once any version leaks, the next is guessable.

### 5.3 The three authentication-factor categories 🟢 — *the backbone concept*

| # | Category | "Something you…" | Examples | Weakness |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Type 1** | 🧠 **Knowledge** | **know** | password, PIN, passphrase | phishable, guessable, reused, shoulder-surfed |
| **Type 2** | 🔑 **Possession** | **have** | phone (OTP/push), OTP token, smart card, **FIDO2 key**, certificate on hardware | lost/stolen; *SMS* is SIM-swappable |
| **Type 3** | 🫆 **Inherence** | **are** (biometric) | fingerprint, face, iris, voice, palm-vein | can't be reissued; spoofable; probabilistic |
| *(context)* | 📍 location / ⌨ behaviour | **are somewhere / do** | geolocation, typing rhythm | supporting signals, not primary |

> 🔑 **MFA rule:** *Multi-Factor* means **two or more factors from *different* categories.** Two passwords = still one factor. Password + PIN = still Type-1 only = **not** MFA. Password + phone-token = Type 1 + Type 2 = **MFA** ✅.

### 5.4 Password alternative #1 — Multi-Factor Authentication (MFA) 🟢

- **Why it works:** an attacker who steals your password *still* can't log in without your *second, independent* factor. MFA turns a credential breach into a non-event.
- **Strength varies hugely by method** (this is the nuance the exam-and-the-field both want):

```
   PHISHABLE ──────────────────────────────► PHISHING-RESISTANT
   SMS OTP  <  TOTP app  <  push approval  <  push+number-match  <  FIDO2 / certificate
   (SIM swap) (better)     (push-fatigue)   (harder)             (cryptographic, origin-bound)
```
- 🔵 **Phishing-resistant MFA** removes the shared secret and binds auth to the *real* site's origin, so a fake site/MITM can't relay it. Certificates and **FIDO2/passkeys** are the two main forms.

### 5.5 Password alternative #2 — Certificates (PKI / X.509) 🟢🔵

- **What it is:** identity proven by a **public/private key pair** wrapped in an **X.509 certificate** signed by a trusted **CA** (Certificate Authority). *(Full PKI/asymmetric-crypto internals live with VPN/IPsec in 9.11.)*
- **How auth happens (challenge-response):** the verifier sends a random challenge; the client **signs** it with its **private key** (which *never leaves* the device/smart card); the verifier checks the signature with the client's **public key** from the cert. **No secret is transmitted** → nothing to phish or replay.
- **Where a network engineer meets it:** **802.1X with EAP-TLS** (certificate-based network access), device identity, SSH public-key auth, HTTPS/mutual-TLS, PIV/smart cards. *(802.1X → 9.5.)*
- **Verdict:** strong and **phishing-resistant**; cost is running a **PKI** (issuance, renewal, revocation via CRL/OCSP).

### 5.6 Password alternative #3 — Biometrics 🟢🔵

- **What it is:** authenticate by a physical/behavioural trait (fingerprint, face, iris, voice).
- **It's probabilistic, not exact** — a fingerprint never scans *identically* twice, so systems use a *match threshold*, which creates two error types:

| Metric | Meaning | Tune it… |
|---|---|---|
| **FAR / FMR** (False Accept Rate) | wrong person **accepted** | ↓ low = **more secure** |
| **FRR** (False Reject Rate) | right person **rejected** | ↓ low = **more usable** |
| **CER / EER** (Crossover/Equal Error Rate) | the point where FAR = FRR | **lower CER = better overall system** |

- **The core trade-off:** tighten the threshold → FAR↓ (safer) but FRR↑ (frustrating); loosen it → opposite. CER is how you compare systems fairly.
- 🔵 **Guardrails (NIST):** require **liveness / Presentation-Attack-Detection** (defeat photos/moulds), FMR ≤ ~1 in 1000, limit failed attempts (~5), prefer **local** matching, and **always pair a biometric with a possession factor** — never use it as the *sole* factor.
- ⚠️ **Biometrics are an identifier, not a secret:** you leave fingerprints everywhere, and you **cannot reissue your face** after a breach. Great as a *local unlock* for a possession factor (Touch ID unlocking a FIDO2 key), risky as a standalone remote credential.

## 6. ⚙️ How It Works — Under the Hood

### 6.1 Why forced rotation *reduces* security (behaviour + entropy)

```
   Policy: "change password every 90 days, must include a symbol"

   User's real behaviour:
     Q1: Summer2024!      ← predictable structure (Word + Year + !)
     Q2: Summer2024!!     ← minimal delta
     Q3: Autumn2024!      ← pattern attackers script for
                          ↑ each new password is DERIVABLE from the last

   If ANY version leaks → attacker guesses the rest in seconds.
   Cognitive load ↑, real entropy ↔ (barely changes), security ↓.

   Modern policy instead:
     one LONG passphrase, screened against breach lists, changed ONLY if compromised
     → higher entropy, lower user pain, no predictable increment to exploit.
```

### 6.2 Why MFA defeats a stolen password (independence)

```
   Attacker has: your password  (phished / breached / sprayed)

   Single-factor login:  password ✔  → 🔓 IN.   ❌ game over.

   MFA login:            password ✔  → prompt for FACTOR 2
                         needs your PHONE / KEY / FINGERPRINT
                         attacker has none of these  → 🔒 blocked.

   The math: compromise probability of factor A × factor B.
   Two *independent* factors ≈ the product of two small numbers = tiny.
   (Independence is the whole point — two passwords aren't independent.)
```

### 6.3 Why certificates & FIDO2 are *phishing-resistant* (no shared secret)

```
   PHISHABLE (shared secret) — password / OTP:
     You → type secret → [fake site relays it] → real site.  Attacker now has the secret. ❌

   PHISHING-RESISTANT (asymmetric) — certificate / FIDO2:
     Verifier → random CHALLENGE ─────────────►  Client
     Client   ◄──── signs challenge w/ PRIVATE KEY (never leaves device) ────
     Verifier verifies signature with PUBLIC KEY.  ✔
       • No secret ever transmitted → nothing to steal or replay.
       • Ceremony is BOUND to the real site's origin/domain → a fake site
         gets a signature it can't reuse anywhere.  ✅ MITM defeated.
```

This is the single most important "why" in modern authentication: **eliminate the shared secret and bind auth to the real relying party.**

### 6.4 The network-engineer's reality on Cisco gear 🔵

Local IOS auth (9.3) is essentially **single-factor password** with thin policy knobs:
```
R1(config)# security passwords min-length 15          ! length floor (management)
R1(config)# login block-for 120 attempts 3 within 60  ! lockout / rate-limit (management)
```
There is **no native "require MFA on the console."** Strong factors reach network devices through **AAA** (9.5):
- **Admin login** → RADIUS/TACACS+ to an identity server that *itself* enforces MFA/cert.
- **Client/endpoint network access** → **802.1X + EAP-TLS** (certificates) or EAP methods that ride MFA.
So 9.4 is the *policy & factor theory*; 9.5 is the *plumbing* that enforces it on the network.

## 7. ⚖️ Pros & Cons

**Passwords (even with strong policy)**
- 👍 Universal, zero extra hardware, cheap, understood by everyone.
- 👎 Phishable, reusable, guessable, breach-prone; the weakest factor by design.

**MFA**
- 👍 Neutralizes stolen passwords; huge risk reduction for low cost; layered.
- 👎 Strength varies wildly (SMS ≪ FIDO2); adds friction; recovery/enrolment complexity; *phishable* forms give false confidence.

**Certificates (PKI)**
- 👍 Strong, **phishing-resistant**, no shared secret, scales to machines & 802.1X, non-repudiation.
- 👎 **PKI is heavy** — issuance, renewal, and **revocation** (CRL/OCSP) are real operational burdens; a compromised CA is catastrophic.

**Biometrics**
- 👍 Convenient, nothing to remember, hard to "share," great *local unlock*.
- 👎 **Not reissuable**, probabilistic (FAR/FRR trade-off), spoofable without liveness, privacy-sensitive; unsafe as a *sole/remote* factor.

## 8. 🧬 Variants (Common → Specific)

- **Password managers** 🔵 — the practical enabler of "long, unique, never-reused" passwords; NIST-aligned policy assumes them.
- **OTP flavours:** **HOTP** (counter-based) vs **TOTP** (time-based, e.g. authenticator apps) vs **SMS/email OTP** (weakest — interceptable/SIM-swap).
- **Push MFA:** simple approve/deny → **number-matching** (defeats push-fatigue) → still not fully phishing-resistant.
- **Possession hardware:** OTP fob → smart card / PIV → **FIDO2 security key** (roaming) → **platform authenticator** (Windows Hello, Touch/Face ID).
- **Certificate use-cases:** user certs (smart card/PIV) · device certs (802.1X EAP-TLS, MDM) · server certs (HTTPS) · mutual TLS.
- **Biometric modalities:** fingerprint → facial geometry → iris → voice → palm-vein → *behavioural* (keystroke/gait).
- **Passwordless / FIDO2 / passkeys** 🔵 — the endgame: possession (device) + inherence (biometric unlock), *no password at all*, phishing-resistant by construction.
- 🔵 **NIST AALs** (Authentication Assurance Levels): **AAL1** (single-factor OK) → **AAL2** (MFA required) → **AAL3** (hardware-based, phishing-resistant, e.g. PIV/FIDO2) — how orgs grade "how strong must auth be here."

## 9. 🧰 Devices / Media / Protocols

- **Identity/MFA servers** — RADIUS/TACACS+ front-ends (ISE, etc.) that enforce policy & MFA for device/user login *(9.5)*.
- **Certificate Authority (CA) / PKI** — issues, renews, and revokes X.509 certs; publishes **CRL/OCSP**.
- **Smart cards / PIV / FIDO2 keys** — possession factors holding private keys in tamper-resistant hardware.
- **Biometric sensors** — fingerprint/face/iris readers; with liveness detection.
- **Protocols & standards:** **802.1X / EAP-TLS** (cert-based network access → 9.5), **RADIUS/TACACS+** (9.5), **TLS/HTTPS & mutual TLS**, **FIDO2/WebAuthn/CTAP**, **X.509**, **OCSP/CRL**.
- **Cisco IOS knobs:** `security passwords min-length`, `login block-for`, `login delay`, `login quiet-mode` — the thin *local* policy layer (mechanism from 9.3).

*(Devices carry no scope markers — universal.)*

## 10. ✅ Best Practices

- 📏 **Length over complexity.** Enforce a solid minimum (**15+** where possible), accept 64+, encourage passphrases; drop mandatory character-mix rules.
- 🚫🔄 **Don't force periodic rotation.** Rotate on *evidence of compromise* only.
- 🧯 **Screen against breach lists** at password creation; reject known-leaked credentials.
- 🔐 **Store strong** (type 8/9 on Cisco; Argon2id/bcrypt/PBKDF2 elsewhere) — length is moot if storage is weak, and strong storage is moot if the password is weak. *Both.*
- ➕ **Require MFA everywhere it matters**, and **prefer phishing-resistant** methods (certificate / FIDO2) for admins and high-value access.
- 🧠🔑 **Combine *different* categories** — password + possession, not password + PIN.
- 🫆 **Biometrics only as a paired/local factor** with liveness detection; never sole, never remote-standalone; remember it can't be reissued.
- 🗂️ **Enable password managers** (don't block paste) — they're how "unique everywhere" becomes real.
- 🏛️ **Grade by assurance (AAL).** Match factor strength to the sensitivity of what's behind the door.
- 🔗 **Enforce on the network via AAA + 802.1X** (9.5); keep a **local break-glass** admin (9.3) for auth-server outages.

## 11. 🚫 No-Goes (Mistakes, Traps & Troubleshooting)

**Common mistakes**
- ❌ **Still mandating 90-day rotation + symbol rules.** Legacy, counter-productive, and now *against* NIST guidance.
- ❌ **Calling two same-category factors "MFA."** Password + security question = **one** factor (both knowledge).
- ❌ **Treating all MFA as equal.** SMS OTP is MFA but phishable/SIM-swappable; don't equate it with FIDO2.
- ❌ **Using a biometric as a *sole*, remote credential** — probabilistic + non-reissuable + spoofable.
- ❌ **Deploying certs without a revocation plan** — a cert you can't revoke is a key you can't take back.
- ❌ **Assuming Cisco devices do native MFA locally** — they don't; it comes through AAA (9.5).
- ❌ **Blocking paste / capping length low** — breaks password managers, forces weaker human passwords.

**🌳 Decision tree — "is this strong auth?"**

```
How many DISTINCT factor categories are in play?
│
├─ 1 (e.g. password only, or password+PIN)  → SINGLE-FACTOR. Weak. Add a 2nd category.
│
└─ 2+ (different categories)  → MFA ✅. Now grade it:
        │
        ├─ Any factor a SHARED SECRET sent over the wire? (password, OTP typed in)
        │        → PHISHABLE MFA. OK-ish; upgrade high-value access.
        │
        └─ Cryptographic, private key never leaves device, origin-bound?
                 (certificate / FIDO2 / passkey)
                 → PHISHING-RESISTANT ✅✅  (AAL3-class). Best.

Biometric present?  → only valid as a PAIRED factor w/ liveness; never sole/remote.
Policy check:  length≥15? breach-screened? no forced rotation? strong hash? → pass/fail each.
```

**🕵️ Dirty Secrets**
- 🤫 **The "strong password rules" everyone learned are officially wrong.** Complexity mandates + 90-day rotation demonstrably *reduce* security by breeding predictable patterns. If a policy still demands `Xx1! every 90 days`, it's cargo-culting 2003 — and you can cite NIST 800-63B Rev 4 to change it.
- 🤫 **Most "MFA" is still phishable.** SMS, TOTP, and plain push all pass a *shared secret / approval* the user can be tricked into handing over (modern phishing kits relay them in real time). Only **cryptographic, origin-bound** factors (certs, FIDO2) actually stop it. "We have MFA" ≠ "we're phishing-resistant."
- 🤫 **Push-fatigue is a real attack.** Spam a user with approval prompts at 2 a.m. until they tap "approve" to make it stop. Number-matching exists specifically because plain push failed this way.
- 🤫 **Your fingerprint is a username, not a password.** It's public (you leave it on every glass), unchangeable, and once a template leaks it's leaked forever. Biometrics shine as a *local unlock* for a hardware key — not as the secret itself.
- 🤫 **PKI's hard part isn't issuing certs — it's revoking them.** CRLs go stale, OCSP responders go down, and a cert you can't reliably revoke is a credential you can't take back. Half of real-world "cert auth" incidents are revocation failures, not crypto failures.
- 🤫 **A password manager quietly satisfies most of NIST at once** (long, unique, never-reused, breach-screenable) — which is why blocking paste (to "be secure") actively makes users *less* secure. The control fights its own goal.
- 🤫 **On Cisco gear, "password policy" is thinner than the exam implies.** `security passwords min-length` + `login block-for` is most of the *local* story; everything richer (MFA, certs, per-command control) is really an **AAA** conversation — which is exactly why 9.5 exists.

## 13. 🧪 Practical Tasks

> 5.4 is a *describe* objective → tasks blend **reasoning drills** with the **thin IOS policy knobs** and a **cert/802.1X preview**.

1. 🧮 **Entropy face-off.** Compare `P@ssw0rd1!` vs `correct-horse-battery-staple` with an offline entropy/zxcvbn estimator; explain why the longer, "simpler" one wins. → *Cements length > complexity.*
2. 🗂️ **Factor-category quiz.** Classify 12 methods (password, PIN, TOTP app, SMS code, YubiKey, fingerprint, smart card, security question, Face ID, push-approve, geolocation, client cert) by Type 1/2/3, then flag which *combinations* are true MFA and which are phishing-resistant. → *Directly rehearses the 5.4 traps.*
3. 🔧 **IOS policy knobs (Packet Tracer 8 / real IOS).** Configure `security passwords min-length 15` and try to set a shorter secret (observe rejection); configure `login block-for 60 attempts 3 within 30`, fail 3 logins, observe lockout; `show login`. → *The concrete local-policy surface.*
4. 🧾 **Write a modern password-policy one-pager** for a fictional org: length, storage, rotation stance, breach-screening, lockout, MFA requirement by data sensitivity (map to AAL1/2/3). Cite NIST 800-63B for each choice. → *Turns theory into a real deliverable.*
5. 🔐 **Certificate challenge-response, on paper.** Diagram an EAP-TLS/CBA login: challenge → sign-with-private-key → verify-with-public-key; annotate *why* no secret crosses the wire and how origin-binding stops MITM. → *Preps the PKI you'll wire up around 9.5/9.11.*
6. 🫆 **FAR/FRR threshold thought-experiment.** Given a biometric with a tunable threshold, argue where you'd set it for (a) a phone unlock vs (b) a data-center door, and justify via CER. → *Makes the security↔usability trade-off concrete.*
